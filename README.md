## 🏆 Final Year Project & Achievement

### Data-Driven Prediction of Fatigue Strength in Heat-Treated 4140 Alloy Steel for Automotive Applications

**Background**

Fatigue strength is an important consideration in automotive materials because repeated loading can lead to material failure. Predicting fatigue strength from composition and processing parameters can help reduce experimental effort and support materials selection.

**What I Did**

- Developed Random Forest and Artificial Neural Network models using Python to predict the fatigue strength of heat-treated 4140 alloy steel.
- Analyzed the influence of chemical composition and heat-treatment parameters on fatigue strength.
- Achieved reported \(R^2\) scores of **0.986 for Random Forest** and **0.971 for ANN** on my project dataset.

**Importance**

The project demonstrated how machine learning can be applied to materials engineering problems and how data-driven models can help investigate relationships between processing conditions and mechanical properties.

**Achievement:** 🥇 Won **1st Prize at Pakistan Auto Show 2024**, organized by the Pakistan Association of Automotive Parts and Accessories Manufacturers (PAAPAM), for presenting this final year project.

## 🚀 AI for Materials Science — Five Projects

### 1. Crystal Graph Convolutional Neural Networks (CGCNN)

**Background**

Crystal structures contain information about atomic arrangements and bonding that influences material properties. CGCNN represents crystals as graphs, enabling machine learning models to learn relationships between crystal structure and properties.

**What I Did**

- Worked with crystal graph-based machine learning for material property prediction.
- Explored the use of crystal structures as model inputs and formation energy as a prediction target.
- Investigated the model workflow for learning structure–property relationships.

**Importance**

This project connects crystallography with AI-based materials modeling. Predicting properties from crystal structures can help screen materials computationally and reduce reliance on expensive calculations or experimental testing.

### 2. Crystal Diffusion Variational Autoencoder (CDVAE)

**Background**

Generative models can learn patterns in known crystal structures and use them to propose new candidate materials. CDVAE combines variational autoencoders and diffusion-based methods for periodic crystal generation.

**What I Did**

- Implemented a generative modeling workflow for crystal structures.
- Worked with perovskite crystal data to explore structure generation.
- Investigated how generated structures can be evaluated as potential material candidates.

**Importance**

Generative materials design shifts AI beyond predicting known properties toward proposing new structures for further investigation. This approach could support the discovery of functional oxides and materials relevant to energy technologies.

### 3. Battery Cycle-Life Prediction Using Machine Learning

**Background**

Battery lifetime is a critical factor in energy storage, affecting reliability, replacement costs, and material waste. Early-cycle measurements may contain useful information for predicting the overall cycle life of a battery.

**What I Did**

- Worked with lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery cycling data.
- Applied data analysis and machine learning to investigate cycle-life prediction from early-cycle measurements.
- Examined the relationship between battery cycling behavior and predicted lifetime.

**Importance**

Reliable lifetime prediction can support battery development, testing strategies, and performance assessment. Longer-lasting batteries can also help reduce replacement frequency and the environmental burden associated with battery production and disposal.

### 4. Sustainable Battery Electrode Screening Using the Materials Project

**Background**

Battery electrode materials must balance electrochemical performance, structural stability, availability, and environmental considerations. Computational databases provide a way to compare candidate materials before experimental validation.

**What I Did**

- Used computational materials data to investigate candidate battery electrode materials.
- Examined properties such as voltage, theoretical capacity, and structural stability.
- Explored sustainability-oriented screening criteria, including reducing dependence on critical raw materials such as cobalt and nickel.

**Importance**

High-throughput computational screening can narrow the search for promising battery materials and help identify alternatives that balance performance with resource sustainability. It connects materials informatics directly with sustainable energy storage.

### 5. Crystal Structure Prediction from Chemical Composition

**Background**

Crystal symmetry influences many physical and chemical properties of materials. Predicting crystal systems from chemical composition provides a data-driven way to investigate relationships between elemental composition and structural characteristics.

**What I Did**

- Developed a machine learning classification workflow using composition-derived features.
- Investigated the prediction of crystal systems from chemical composition.
- Evaluated model predictions to understand classification performance across different structural categories.

**Importance**

Composition-based prediction can help prioritize candidate materials for further structural analysis and computational investigation. It provides a useful starting point for data-driven crystallography and materials discovery.
